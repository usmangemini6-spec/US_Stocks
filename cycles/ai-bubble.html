Is the AI bubble bursting?

Written on 20 November 2026; updated when the situation changes.

Short answer: a sharp correction is under way; a full burst is not confirmed yet.

All three US indexes are now in a correction. The S&P 500 closed at 6,910 on 20 Nov, 11.6% below its 6 Oct record, and the NASDAQ is 18.1% below its record, close to the 20% line of a bear market. The PHLX chip index was already 38% below its June record on 5 Nov. A burst would mean the AI spending itself is cut; so far no large cloud company has cut its spending plans.

Signs of a bubble

  • Concentration: more than half of the ~$32 trillion added to the S&P 500 since ChatGPT came from just 10 AI-linked companies.
  • Spending: the five largest cloud companies are spending about $800 billion in 2026, and AI spending drives about half of S&P 500 earnings growth this year.
  • Sentiment turning: fund managers cut tech by the most since 2006 in November; 52% expect at least one big cloud company to cut spending within six months.
  • Expert warnings: one well-known bubble researcher now puts the chance of an AI bubble at 70%.
  • Triggers this month: NVIDIA's CEO exit and a reported SEC inquiry, worries about data-center rules after the midterm elections, and inflation stuck near 4%.

Signs it is not (yet) a burst

  • Profits are real: NVIDIA beat its own forecast again on 17 Nov and guided higher; earnings keep beating estimates.
  • Spending not cut: Alphabet kept its 2026 plan of $195–205 billion, and 2027 plans are reported to be higher.
  • Central banks: a US Fed governor said on 18 Nov there is nothing “bubble-like” in how AI is priced.
  • Size of the fall: −11.6% is a normal correction; the S&P 500 has had 27 corrections since 1928, most of which did not turn into bear markets.

Is the S&P 500 stretched? Price vs its 10-year average

How far the S&P 500 sits above the average of its previous 10 years, monthly since 1950. Past bubble peaks: +131% in 1999 (dot-com) and +117% in 1987; +94% at the end of 2021. In August 2026 it reached +90%; at the 20 Nov close it is about +68%.

Reading: high but below the 1987 and 2000 peaks. This measure says US stocks are expensive, not that a crash must follow; in 2021 a similar level was followed by a 25% fall in 2022.

What the cycles add

2026 is the midterm year (year 2 of the presidential cycle). Historically its low comes around Sep–Oct and the following 12 months, into the pre-election year, are the strongest of the four. This fall is arriving about a month later than usual. In the 2000–2002 dot-com burst the market kept falling for two and a half years, but its final low still came in the midterm year (October 2002).

Suggestion at this point

Do not buy all at once. If you are adding to US stocks, spread buys over the next 2–3 months (for example in three parts), favour companies with strong free cash flow and net cash over the most crowded AI names, and keep cash for a deeper fall if the NASDAQ breaks −20%. Reduce if large cloud companies start cutting their 2027 spending. The cycles favour buying in this midterm-year weakness; the AI news decides how deep it goes.
